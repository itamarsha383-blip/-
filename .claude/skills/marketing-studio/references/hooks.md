# Hooks — the first 3 seconds

A hook is the opening line or first frame that stops the scroll. It is the single
highest-leverage element in any piece of content: a mediocre body with a great hook
outperforms a great body with a weak hook, because nobody reads the body they
scrolled past. Always generate **10–15** and pick the strongest — quantity then
selection beats trying to nail one.

## The mechanics of a stopping hook
A hook works by opening a gap the brain needs to close, or by hitting something the
viewer already cares about. Front-load it: the first **3 words** should carry
tension, specificity, or a callout. Never open with setup, greetings, logos, or
"in this video I'm going to...".

Vary the mechanism across your 15 so they don't all sound the same:
- **Curiosity gap** — tease an outcome, withhold the how.
- **Bold / contrarian claim** — say the thing that makes people stop to argue.
- **Direct callout** — name the exact person this is for.
- **Specific number / result** — concreteness reads as credible.
- **Open loop / tension** — start mid-story at the most charged moment.
- **Question** — but only one they *must* answer in their head.
- **Negativity / mistake** — what they're doing wrong; loss aversion is strong.

## Hook formulas (fill in the blanks)
- "The [thing] nobody tells you about [topic]."
- "Stop doing [common thing] if you want [desired outcome]."
- "I [got result] in [timeframe]. Here's exactly how."
- "You're [doing thing] wrong. Here's the fix."
- "[Audience]: this is why [problem] keeps happening."
- "3 [things] that [big benefit] (number 2 shocked me)."
- "If you [situation], watch this before you [action]."
- "The fastest way to [outcome] — no [common requirement] needed."
- "I tried [thing] for [timeframe]. This is what happened."
- "Nobody's talking about [surprising truth], so I will."
- "How to [outcome] even if [common objection]."
- "This one [small thing] changed my [big result]."

## Worked examples
**Weak:** "Hi everyone, today I want to share some tips about saving money."
**Strong:** "You're broke because of 3 subscriptions you forgot you had."
> Callout + specific number + mild accusation = the brain needs the list.

**Weak:** "Our new coffee blend is now available."
**Strong:** "I quit my $6 café habit. This tastes better and costs 40 cents."
> Personal + specific numbers + a contrarian value claim.

**Weak:** "Some thoughts on leadership."  (LinkedIn)
**Strong:** "I fired my best performer. It was the right call."
> Tension + taboo + promise of a lesson → the "see more" tap.

## Pressure-test every hook before you ship it
- Would a stranger who doesn't know you stop for this?
- Is it specific, or could it sit on anyone's post?
- Does it open a gap the body actually pays off? (Never clickbait a payoff you
  don't deliver — it kills trust and retention.)
- Is the first frame/line readable in under a second?

Recommend your top 3 to the user with one line each on *why* it pulls. Teach the
mechanism as you go — that's how a beginner starts writing their own.
