# Audience psychology — the levers that make people stop, feel, and act

Content doesn't move people because it's clever; it moves them because it hits
something they already feel. Use these levers deliberately in Phase 1 (positioning)
and Phase 4 (the draft). The goal is never manipulation — it's clarity and resonance:
saying the true thing in the way that actually lands.

## Start from the audience, not the product
Beginners describe *what they made*. Pros describe *what the audience feels*. Before
writing anything, answer:
- **The pain**: what frustrates, scares, or embarrasses them right now?
- **The desire**: what do they secretly want (status, ease, belonging, safety, time)?
- **Their words**: the exact phrases they'd use — mirror those, not corporate speak.
- **The enemy**: what/who is to blame? People rally around a shared "against".

Write to **one specific person**, not a demographic. "New moms who feel judged for
using screen time" produces sharper content than "parents".

## The core levers
- **Curiosity gap** — an open loop the brain wants closed. Drives the click and the
  watch-through. Open it fast; pay it off honestly.
- **Emotion over information** — people share what makes them *feel* (awe, anger,
  laughter, inspiration, being seen). Facts inform; emotion spreads.
- **Specificity = credibility** — "$6 latte", "3 subscriptions", "in 11 days" feel
  true. Round, vague claims feel like marketing.
- **Loss aversion** — "what you're losing / doing wrong" often beats "what you'd
  gain". Frame the cost of inaction.
- **Social proof** — results, numbers, testimonials, "10,000 people". We copy what
  others already validated.
- **Identity & belonging** — "this is for people like us" content makes the viewer
  want to signal membership by sharing.
- **Status** — content that makes the sharer look smart/ahead/caring gets shared.
- **Story** — a real before→struggle→after is the most retained structure there is.
  Tension is what holds attention; resolution is what satisfies.
- **The "aha"** — teach something genuinely useful; usefulness earns saves and follows.

## Awareness stages (match the message to where they are)
Don't sell a solution to someone who doesn't know they have the problem.
1. **Unaware** — doesn't know they have the problem → lead with the problem/story.
2. **Problem-aware** — feels the pain, doesn't know solutions → agitate + reveal
   there's a way.
3. **Solution-aware** — knows solutions exist, comparing → show why yours, proof.
4. **Product-aware** — knows you → offer, urgency, remove friction.
Cold social audiences are mostly stages 1–2. Email/retargeting is 3–4.

## Turn attention into action
- **One CTA.** A confused viewer does nothing. Ask for exactly one thing.
- **Make it effortless** — "comment WORD", "save this", "tap the link". Lower the
  cost of the next step.
- **Give a reason** — "save this so you don't lose it", "follow for part 2".
- **Reciprocity** — give real value first; the ask lands better after you've helped.

## The honesty line
Every lever above works best when the underlying thing is true. Curiosity you don't
pay off, proof you don't have, urgency you invent — these get short-term clicks and
long-term distrust, and platforms punish low retention. World-class marketing is
making something genuinely good impossible to ignore, not tricking people into bad
content.
